  <Step title="Asked with no sources" stage="a">
    We asked the model the question of the day, with no sources. It said "depending on the company's policy". That sounds careful. But it still gave numbers: 25-30 working days. The model never saw Kraken Air's policy. So these numbers don't come from there.
  </Step>
  <Step title="How did it write that?" stage="a">
    The model writes the answer <Term id="token">token</Term> by token. At each step, it picks the most likely next token for the text so far. After "How many working days", texts usually go on with a number. No step checks that the number is right.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Where does the knowledge live?" stage="b">
    Everything the model knows is in its <Term id="weights">weights</Term>. In gemma3:4b, that is about 4 billion numbers. Training changes these numbers in tiny steps, so the model predicts text better. The knowledge is not in one place. It is spread over all the numbers.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Training ended, the numbers froze" stage="b">
    When training ends, the weights freeze. Every question reads them, and no question writes to them. Chatting doesn't teach the model anything either. Kraken Air's policy was not in the training data. So it is not in these numbers.
  </Step>
  <Step title="It doesn't know that it doesn't know" stage="a">
    The policy says 22 working days. That is not even inside the model's range. The model hedged, but it did not say "I don't know". No warning light comes on inside it. A made-up answer sounds as fluent as a right one. This is called a <Term id="hallucination">hallucination</Term>.

    <Now>Another question with no sources: `npm run step -- 0 "How many days do I have to upload my receipts after a trip?"`</Now>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Two ways to give the model knowledge" stage="b">
    We can retrain the model on our own examples and change its weights. That is <Term id="finetune">fine-tuning</Term>. Or, with every question, we put the related text in front of the model. That is <Term id="rag">RAG</Term>. We try RAG first. Fine-tuning comes back at the end of this section.
  </Step>
</Deck>

## Common questions

<FAQ q="Does any model say “I don’t know” with no setup?">
Not reliably. Some models sometimes don't answer, but you can't know in advance when.
In RAG, you **design** "I don't know". How we design it: [F · Generating the answer](/en/adim-adim/f-cevap/).
</FAQ>

<FAQ q="Does a bigger model know?">
On public topics, it knows more. But Kraken Air is fictional, and no model has a company's internal documents in
its training data. On this question, a big model also makes something up or stays silent. You can't choose which.
</FAQ>

<FAQ q="Doesn’t it learn while we chat?">
No. The earlier messages go into the prompt again every time. That is why it seems to remember. When the chat
ends, nothing stays, and the weights don't change.
</FAQ>

<Takeaway>It doesn't know, and it doesn't know that it doesn't know.</Takeaway>
