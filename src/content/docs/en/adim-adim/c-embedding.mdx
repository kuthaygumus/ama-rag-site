  <Step title="Text goes in, 1024 numbers come out" stage="v">
    `4-embed.ts` sends each chunk to the bge-m3 model. It gets back a list of 1024 numbers, a <Term id="vektor">vector</Term>. This vector is the text's <Term id="embedding">embedding</Term>.

    <Now n="1">Run `npm run step -- 4`, then open `data/4-vectors.json`.</Now>
  </Step>
  <Step title="One number alone means nothing" stage="v">
    No single one of the 1024 numbers means "leave" or "date". The meaning is in where two vectors sit, compared to each other. Close meaning, close vectors.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Who decided what is close?" stage="v">
    Not us. The model. bge-m3 is a neural network. Its training used many examples of "these two texts say the same thing". In training, it learned which texts to put close together. Its weights are frozen now.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The question goes through the same model" stage="v">
    The question also becomes 1024 numbers. Without that, we can't compare it with the chunks. The model must be the same. Another model puts its vectors in another space.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Measuring closeness: cosine" stage="m" phase="1">
    It compares two vectors by the angle between them. It ignores length and looks only at direction. The result is between −1 and 1, and 1 means the same direction. Our retriever and our Chroma collection both use it: <Term id="cosine">cosine similarity</Term>.
  </Step>
  <Step title="L2: the distance between the tips" stage="m" phase="2">
    The straight-line distance between two points. Careful: the scale runs the other way. A small number means close.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Dot: multiply and add" stage="m" phase="3">
    Dot multiplies the matching numbers and adds them up. Take a = (3, 0) and b = (2, 2): 3×2 + 0×2 = 6. That is all the code does. Unlike cosine, it doesn't divide by the lengths.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Dot in the picture: a shadow" stage="m" phase="3">
    Draw a line from the tip of b to a, at a right angle. b's shadow on a is 2. Length of a times the shadow: 3 × 2 = 6 again. Our vectors have length 1.000 (`4-embed.ts` log). So what difference is left between dot and cosine?

    <Now n="2">Write the empty `dot()` function in `src/lib/similarity.ts`. Check: `npm run similar -- --metric dot`. Each pair should show a number, not an error.</Now>
  </Step>
</Deck>

## Common questions

<FAQ q="What does similar.ts do?">
It is a tool for looking, not a step of the pipeline. It sends two texts to the embedding model. Then it prints how close their vectors are. It has five ready pairs, and one of them is a trap. The pairs teach the model nothing. The model is fixed, and no other step reads these pairs. If the trap fails today, it fails tomorrow too. Only a different model, or a later check like rerank, changes that.
</FAQ>

<FAQ q="Why does dot give the same result as cosine?">
Because bge-m3 vectors have length 1.000. Cosine divides the dot result by the lengths of the two vectors. Dividing by 1 changes nothing. So many vector DBs offer dot as a "fast cosine" for vectors of length 1.
</FAQ>

<FAQ q="What do dense and sparse mean?">
Our vectors are dense. All 1024 numbers are filled, and together they carry the meaning. A sparse vector has one dimension for each word in a vocabulary, and most of them are zero. That is word matching (the BM25 family). Hybrid search combines the two.
</FAQ>

<FAQ q="Which embedding model should I pick?">
Check its language support (we need English and Turkish). Check the vector size, because you store one vector for each chunk. Check how long a text it can read. Check where it runs: locally or through an API? That decides where your data goes. Public leaderboards give you an idea. But you decide by measuring on your own question set (Measuring quality).
</FAQ>

<FAQ q="What happens if I change the model?">
You re-embed every chunk. The old vectors stay in a different space. The code stops you from mixing them. `4-embed.ts` writes the model name into `data/4-vectors.json`. If `6-retrieve.ts` sees a different model for the question, it stops. It asks you to run `4-embed.ts` and `5-store.ts` again.
</FAQ>

<FAQ q="Are cosine, L2 and dot different methods?">
Yes. They are three ways to measure closeness. Cosine looks at the angle. L2 measures the distance between the tips, so smaller means closer. Dot multiplies the numbers and adds them up. Our vectors all have length 1, so all three give the same order. With vectors of different lengths, they can disagree.
</FAQ>

<Takeaway>The model decides what is close, not the database.</Takeaway>
