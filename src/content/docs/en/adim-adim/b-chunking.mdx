  <Step title="Why do we split?" stage="a">
    Sending all 8 documents with every question costs a lot. Most of it is not about the question. So we split them into pieces. The search picks the few pieces closest to the question. These pieces are called <Term id="chunk">chunks</Term>.
  </Step>
  <Step title="First way: cut every 300 characters" stage="b">
    `fixedSize(doc, 300)` cuts every 300 characters. It doesn't look at the content. It doesn't see headings, tables or even words. The code is a short function, and everyone starts with this one. 300 is our choice, not a rule.

    <Now n="1">In `src/steps/3-chunk.ts`, set the chunker to `fixed`. Then run `npm run step -- 3`.</Now>
  </Step>
  <Step title="The cut lands in the table" stage="b">
    c06 cuts the heading word "Annual" in two: "Ann" in c06, "ual" in c07. c07 ends the row for year 5 with "| 5 | 2". c08 starts with "2 |".

    <Now n="2">Open `data/3-chunks.json`. Find `hr-leave@2025#c07` and `hr-leave@2025#c08`. Which one has the table header row?</Now>
  </Step>
  <Step title="What does a row say without its header?" stage="b">
    The answer row `| 7 | 22 |` is in c08. Its header row stayed in c07. So c08 does not say what 7 and 22 count. The word "annual" is not there either.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The model answers anyway" stage="b">
    c08 is not among the 3 sources. The model answers:

    "An employee with 7 years of service gets at most 7 working days [1]."

    Source [1] is c11. It starts with "at most 7 working days." That sentence starts in c10: "one block of leave can be". The cut hid what the 7 counts. A source, but a wrong number.

    <Now n="3">Run `npm run question`.</Now>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Second way: cut at the document's own headings" stage="b">
    `bySection(doc)` cuts at every `## n.` heading. It puts `[Annual Leave Policy › 3. Annual Paid Leave]` in front of each chunk. The table stays in the same chunk as the heading that explains it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Same question, right answer" stage="b">
    With section chunks, the model answers: "An employee with 7 years of service gets 22 working days of annual leave [1]." The source is `hr-leave@2025#3`. Only one thing changed: where we cut.

    <Now n="4">Switch the chunker back to `section`. Run `npm run step -- 3`, then `npm run question` again.</Now>
  </Step>
  <Step title="The cost: bigger chunks, a shared prefix" stage="b">
    Section: 60 chunks, 695 characters on average. Fixed: 138 chunks, 294 on average. Bigger chunks fill the prompt faster. The `[Document › Section]` prefix is like page A's header, but it carries meaning. Chunks of one document look more alike (page E).
  </Step>

  <DeckFigure slot="figure">

| Way to cut | How it cuts | Good for | Watch out | In our repo |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fixed size | Every N characters | A quick start, any text | Cuts tables, sentences, even words | Yes: `fixed` |
| By section | At the document's own headings | Documents with clear headings | A long section makes a big chunk | Yes: `section` |
| By paragraph | At blank lines, packed up to a size | Text with few headings | A piece can lose its heading | Yes: `paragraph`, you write it |
| Recursive splitter | Big separators first, smaller ones only when a piece is too long | Mixed documents | You choose the separators and the size | No |
| By sentence | At the end of each sentence | Short facts, FAQ pages | One sentence alone often says too little | No |
| Semantic | Where the meaning changes between sentences | Long text with no structure | An embedding for every sentence, hard to predict | No |
| Parent-child | Search small chunks, give the model the big chunk around them | Exact search and full context | Two sizes to store and link, a longer prompt | No |

  </DeckFigure>
  <Step title="Many ways to cut">
    Fixed and section are two ways to cut. The table shows the common ones. Three of them are in our repo. The third one, by paragraph, is empty. You write it today.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Overlap is a setting, not a way">
    Overlap is not a way to cut. You can add it to any way in the table. Neighbor chunks share a few sentences. So an idea at the cut has a better chance to stay whole. The cost: more chunks and repeated text.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Recursive: big cuts first">
    A recursive splitter tries big separators first: sections, then paragraphs, then sentences. It goes smaller only when a piece is too long. Our `bySection` is a small one. It cuts at headings first. A section over 1500 characters is cut again at its paragraphs.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Parent-child: find small, read big">
    <Term id="parentchild">Parent-child</Term> keeps chunks in two sizes. The search uses the small ones, so it can find one exact row. The model gets the big one around it, so it sees the heading too. We don't build it today.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Which way is best?">
    It depends on your documents and your questions. So don't guess. Measure with `npm run eval`, the same exam for every way. Now you write the third way, and we measure it.

    <Now n="5">Write `byParagraph` in `src/steps/3-chunk.ts`. Check it with `npm run check:sibling`.</Now>
  </Step>

## Common questions

<FAQ q="Why 300? Would another number save the table?">
Sometimes. Another number moves the cut to another place. We tried other sizes on this document. At 250, 350, 450, 500 and 1000 characters, the header row stays with the `| 7 |` row. A cut every 300 characters doesn't. We use 300 here because it is a size that splits the table. The problem stays the same: the cut doesn't look at the content. With any number, some cut lands inside a table or a sentence.
</FAQ>

<FAQ q="What if the chunks overlap a little?">
That's a common method. Neighbor chunks share a few hundred characters. A heading at the cut sometimes lands in the next chunk too. There's no guarantee, and you get more chunks and more storage. We didn't measure this on our corpus.
</FAQ>

<FAQ q="What if the document has no headings?">
You can split at paragraphs (YOUR TURN 5), at sentences, or where the meaning changes (semantic chunking). Don't guess which one wins. Measure it: on the Measuring quality page, `npm run eval` compares the two chunkers on the same question set.
</FAQ>

<FAQ q="Is a bigger chunk always better?">
No. A bigger chunk makes the prompt longer, so it costs more and takes longer. It also mixes different topics in one vector. So `bySection` splits a section over 1500 characters again, at its paragraphs (`MAX_SECTION_CHARS`, a limit we chose).
</FAQ>

<FAQ q="What does sections: 2,3 mean on a fixed chunk?">
When a cut crosses a section heading, the chunk touches two sections at once. The `sections` field records this. The eval (Measuring quality) checks the right answer at the section level, so this field matters.
</FAQ>

<Takeaway>The chunker made this difference, not the model.</Takeaway>
