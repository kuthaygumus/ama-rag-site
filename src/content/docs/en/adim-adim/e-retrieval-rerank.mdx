  <Step title="The question becomes a vector too" stage="list">
    Step 6 embeds the question and asks Chroma for the 5 closest chunks. The filter runs inside the search: only the 2025 edition, only documents open to everyone.
  </Step>
  <Step title="What did the retriever return?" stage="list">
    5 chunks, all from the Annual Leave Policy. §3, the leave table with the answer, comes first.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The scores are almost equal" stage="list">
    First: 0.614. Fifth: 0.598. The gap is only 0.016. Every hr-leave chunk starts with `[Annual Leave Policy › …]`. The question also says "annual leave". That may pull all five close. We didn't test this. §3 is first, but only by a little.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The cutoff: k" stage="list">
    Taking the k closest chunks is called <Term id="topk">top-k</Term>. Here k = 5, so benefits §6 in 6th place (0.585) stays out. A small k can miss the right chunk. A large k sends more noise to the next steps.

    <Now n="1">Run `npm run step -- 6`, then open `data/6-retrieved.json`.</Now>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Only 3 chunks go into the prompt" stage="list">
    With rerank off, the top 3 by vector go in: §3, §2 and §7. §2 is about probation. §7 is about carrying leave over. Neither answers the question, but both take room in the prompt.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Rerank: read the question and the chunk together" stage="list">
    We made each vector alone: the question, then each chunk. A <Term id="rerank">reranker</Term> scores each pair of question and chunk from 0 to 10. That is rel in the log. Here our chat model gemma3:4b does it: 5 chunks, 5 calls.
  </Step>
  <Step title="After rerank" stage="list">
    §3 stays first with 10. The rest get 6 or less. The 0.016 gap is now a clear split. §2 gets 3 and drops out. §7 and §4, the next best, take places 2 and 3. Neither has the answer.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The cost" stage="list">
    The search itself took 8 ms (the whole step, 22 ms). Rerank took 1498 ms. For this question, the answer didn't change: 22 working days in both runs. Rerank matters when the right chunk is not in the top 3.

    <Now n="2">In `src/steps/7-rerank.ts`, turn rerank off. Run `npm run step -- 7` and `npm run step -- 8`.</Now>
  </Step>
  <Step title="The right chunk is not always in front" stage="list">
    Now use other words: "How much holiday do I get? I started here 4 years ago." The leave policy never says "holiday". It says "annual leave". By vector, the leave table is only 5th, so it misses the prompt. Rerank lifts it to rank 1. The answer: 18 working days.

    <Now n="3">Run `npm run ask -- "How much holiday do I get? I started here 4 years ago."`. What rank is the leave table by vector, and what rank after rerank?</Now>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Top-k is not the only way" stage="yol">
    A threshold keeps chunks above a score. On page C, even the "unrelated" pair scored 0.503. Set it on your data. <Term id="mmr">MMR</Term> skips near-copies. Hybrid adds keyword search (BM25). This repo has top-k and the filter. The rest are ideas.

  </Step>

## Common questions

<FAQ q="How do I pick k?">
By measuring. `npm run eval` counts how many questions have the right section in the top k (recall@k). See [Measuring quality](/en/uretim/kaliteyi-olcmek/). Our retriever gives 5 candidates, and 3 go into the prompt (`topK` and `contextK`, `src/lib/config.ts`). Both numbers are our choice.
</FAQ>

<FAQ q="Do I need rerank for every question?">
No. If the retriever already puts the right chunk in the top 3, the rerank time is wasted. The eval shows how many questions need it.
</FAQ>

<FAQ q="Can the reranker be wrong?">
Yes. For the salary question, it gave a score of 10 to both travel tables: international (EUR) and domestic (TRY). Ties happen too. The `ties` count in the log shows how many chunks got the same score.
</FAQ>

<FAQ q="What do bi-encoder and cross-encoder mean?">
The embedding model is a bi-encoder. It turns the question and the chunk into vectors separately. We compute the chunk vectors in advance, so the search is fast. A cross-encoder reads both together. It is more careful, but it runs again for every candidate of every question. Our reranker is a chat model, but it uses the same idea. Special cross-encoder models do this job faster. We didn't measure them.
</FAQ>

<Takeaway>Finding the right chunk is not enough. It must also rank high enough to get into the prompt.</Takeaway>
